## Talking points
- **The house divides.** Rehoboam and Jeroboam, 931 BC. Israel in the north has every king evil. Judah in the south has a mix.
- **The prophets are the tell.** God sends warning after warning. Scripture gives the reason: "because he had compassion on his people and on his dwelling place" (2 Chronicles 36:15).
- **The glory leaves slowly.** In Ezekiel it lifts, pauses at the threshold, moves to the east gate, pauses, then stops on the mountain east of the city. It's like someone who keeps turning around.
- **The walls fall.** Assyria takes Israel (722 BC). Babylon takes Judah (587 BC). The lowest point on the arc is exile, not the Fall.
- **The takeaway.** God is willing to lose the Temple but not His people.

## Questions for the class
- Why does the glory leave slowly instead of all at once?
- God kept sending messengers "because he had compassion." How can a warning be an act of love?
- God let the Temple fall but kept the people. What does that tell you about which one He cared about more?

## The storylines here
- **Father:** Warning after warning, and then exile as a Father's correction. In Fr. Tadros Malaty's words, "He chastens, not to avenge, but to mend and renew!"
- **Teacher:** In Babylon there is no Temple and no altar. The pictures stop, and the people have to remember what they meant.
- **Neighbor:** The glory departs from the house. But God is still waiting for one soul in captivity to make into a sanctuary.

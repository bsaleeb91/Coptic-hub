## Talking points
- **God comes down.** Israel can't climb up to God, so God comes down: the blood of the lamb, the water of the sea, the Law at Sinai, and then a tent pitched in the middle of the camp.
- **The thesis.** "Let them make me a sanctuary, that I may dwell amongst them" (Exodus 25:8). The whole point of the Law and the tabernacle is that God wants to live with His people.
- **The golden calf.** While Moses is still on the mountain receiving the Law, the people make an idol. Moses' answer is not "give us the land anyway." It is "If your presence doesn't go with me, don't carry us up from here" (Exodus 33:15). He would rather stay in the desert with God than enter the promised land without Him.
- **Egypt in the heart.** You can take the people out of Egypt without getting Egypt out of the people. The wilderness shows what was still inside them.
- **The judges.** The same cycle, over and over, until Samuel: the last judge and the first prophet.

## Questions for the class
- Israel was rescued in a single night but took forty years to change. Why is transformation so much slower than rescue?
- Moses chose God's presence over the promised land. What does that tell you about what he wanted most?
- What "Egypt" might a person still carry around after God has rescued them?

## The storylines here
- **Father:** The judges cycle is the Father storyline in miniature: God loves, man sins, man repents, God forgives, on repeat.
- **Teacher:** The Passover lamb and the Levitical system. Blood on the doorposts is a picture before it is a rule.
- **Neighbor:** The tabernacle stands in the middle of the camp. For the first time since Eden, God has a house among His people.

The weight of shepherding

You carry them all.
Every single one.

A Coptic priest doesn't have 10 spiritual children. He has 150. Some seasons, 200. Each one carries their own burdens — a struggling marriage, a faith that's growing cold, a sin that keeps returning.

He remembers faces. He prays for names. But the details — the canon he assigned in January, the prayer request shared in confidence, the person who hasn't come to confession since Lent — those are harder to hold.

He relies on a notebook. His memory. The rare moment someone reaches out first. It is not enough — not because he doesn't care, but because no human mind was built to shepherd at this scale without a tool.

What gets missed today

✝

The drifting child

Someone confesses a deep struggle in February. By May they've stopped coming. The priest finds out months later — or never. There was no signal, no alert, no way to know.

📜

The forgotten canon

You assigned 40 days of the Agpeya at the end of confession. You have no way to follow up. The canon lives in a notebook and dies there.

◇

The unheard prayer

A spiritual child shares a private prayer request — a sick parent, a marriage in trouble. Between confessions there is no channel. No way to follow up.



Introducing Poimen


No one falls
through the cracks.

Poimen is a pastoral care companion — not a productivity app, not a CRM, not a generic tool repurposed for ministry. Every feature was designed around a single relationship: the Father of Confession and his spiritual child.

"The Good Shepherd leaves the 99 to find the one. Poimen tells him which one."

The Sunday School Servant

Confession Preparation

Come to confession
prepared.

The examination of conscience guides you through four areas — toward God, toward others, toward self, toward the Church. Checkboxes. Text notes. A quiet space to be honest before you are honest with your priest.

What you write exists only in this session. When you close the screen, it is gone. Not encrypted. Not anonymized. Gone. The seal of confession begins before you step into the room.

  • Guided examination across four spiritual dimensions
  • Free-text notes for the details only you and God need to see
  • Session-only — no data is written to any server, ever
  • View your last confession date and assigned canon as a reminder
  • No account of your sins goes to a database. Not one line.

Spiritual Journal & Vitals

A private place
to tend your soul.

The journal is yours. Write about your prayer life, your struggles, what moved you in the liturgy. Add tags. Reflect on a scripture. No one reads it unless you invite them to.

Spiritual vitals let you share a snapshot of your inner life with your Father of Confession — prayer frequency, scripture reading, fasting, liturgy attendance. You control the dial. Share it or keep it. His care becomes more personal when he knows where you are.

  • Private journal — entries, tags, scripture references
  • Share a single entry with your FOC with one tap
  • Spiritual vitals: self-rated across five dimensions
  • Vitals are only shared when you explicitly choose to send them
  • Your FOC sees a snapshot — not a live feed, not a log

Prayer Requests & Pastoral Timeline

Stay connected
between confessions.

Share a prayer request directly with your Father of Confession — without waiting until your next confession. He sees it in his member view. He prays for it. He can follow up.

Your pastoral timeline shows you what your priest has shared back — the note from your last confession, the blessing from a home visit, the canon he assigned and why. The relationship doesn't end when you leave the room.

  • Prayer requests — private (only you) or shared with your FOC
  • Your FOC sees only what you explicitly send him
  • Pastoral timeline: a record of your journey together
  • Encounter notes from your priest appear here when he chooses to share them
  • A living record of the relationship, visible to both sides

Privacy is a theological commitment.

The holy seal of confession is absolute. Poimen was designed from the ground up to honor it — not as a legal compliance matter, but as a matter of faith.

🔒

Confession content never stored

The examination of conscience and all preparation notes exist only in the session. They are never written to a database, never transmitted, never cached. When the screen closes, they are gone.

✝

Only the date is recorded

When a priest logs a confession, only the date and encounter type are saved — matching what he already writes in a notebook. Nothing more. The seal is honored in every line of code.

◈

Sharing is always a choice

Spiritual vitals, prayer requests, journal entries — none are shared with the priest unless the congregant explicitly chooses to share them. The default is private. Always.

"What you bring to God stays between you and God. Poimen exists to support that relationship — not to observe it."
